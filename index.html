Guide

Pick an element or compound, then draw into the chamber. Every cell carries a real temperature in kelvin, and behaviour comes from each element's measured properties.

Controls

  • Left drag: use the current tool. Right drag: orbit. Middle drag or Shift + right drag: pan. Wheel: zoom.
  • Ctrl+Z undo, Ctrl+Y redo. P pins the cell under the cursor and graphs its temperature.
  • ← → ↑ ↓ turn the view, + − zoom, Home resets it. Middle double-click, or Alt + double-click, centres the view on what is under the pointer.
  • * or the star on the data card keeps a material in the bar under the chamber. The Compounds tab can be narrowed to acids, salts, organics and more.
  • [ ] brush size. 1–9 tools. Space pause. T hide the table. H heat view.
  • Height sets how far above the surface you pour. Set it to 0 to paint directly on surfaces.
  • Place at sets the starting temperature, so you can pour liquid nitrogen at 70 K or molten iron at 2000 K.

Lab equipment

  • Press B or choose Lab. Pick a container, a hot plate or a chiller in the panel, then click a surface to set it down.
  • Drag to carry. Wheel tips a carried container so it pours; Shift + wheel lifts it. Q and E turn the spout. F fills it with the selected material.
  • A container that sits still becomes real glass in the chamber, and what is inside it reacts, heats and boils like anything else.

Saving

  • Save and Load use files, including your equipment. The game also keeps an automatic save of the current chamber and offers it the next time you start. Settings can turn that off.

What is real

  • Melting and boiling points, density by phase, specific heat, thermal conductivity, latent heats, electronegativity, ionisation energy, oxidation states, electrode potentials, electrical conductivity, Curie points, superconducting temperatures, neutron cross-sections, half-lives and decay modes.
  • Compound formulas come from real oxidation states. Common compounds use measured data; the rest are estimated and marked est.
  • The Reactions tab lists every equation in the database, each one checked for atom balance. Click one to run it in a small test rig. Reaction heat comes from heats of formation wherever they are known.
  • Gibbs energy decides how far a reaction can go at a given temperature and pressure, from measured enthalpies and entropies where all are known. Reversible reactions settle at their real equilibrium side.
  • Cells hold true moles from density and formula mass. Elements carry isotopes, with real half-lives and decay modes. Water cells report pH, with buffers and solubility products.
  • Beyond the database, families of rules cover every metal: acids with bases, carbonates and oxides, thermal decomposition, precipitation by solubility rules, and displacement by electrode potential.
  • Gas density follows the ideal gas law, so hot gas rises and xenon sinks. Heating curves plateau at phase changes because latent heat is banked.

What is simplified

  • Half-lives are compressed on a log scale so that a 14-billion-year nuclide and a millisecond one can both be watched. Their order is preserved.
  • Heat conduction is compressed by a square root, so copper and water can both move at a visible pace. Latent heats run at half value.
  • Each voxel is one bulk lump of material, not an atom. Gases are treated as a cell of condensed-density material, which is far denser than a real gas. Air is an endless oxygen supply.
  • Activation energies are derived from each reaction's onset temperature, not measured. Thermodynamic values are at 298 K and do not change with temperature, apart from water turning to steam.
  • Dissolved salts are tracked as salts that behave as their ions, not as separate ions. pH is calculated for at most two solutes in a cell, ignoring activity effects and metal-ion acidity.
  • Pressure is a simple spreading field in kPa, without a full fluid solver. Burst strength is a rough estimate from melting point.
  • Fission chances per isotope are simplified, and the small grid needs fuel far richer than real reactors. Fusion is a simplified path up the binding-energy curve.
  • For elements past about Z = 100, bulk properties have never been measured. Those values are theoretical predictions, marked est.
